A tour

What using it actually looks like

  1. Two apps in one, and the notch flips them

    The pill at the top of the screen shows the side you're on, plus a stat from the other one. Tap it and the whole app flips between Lifting and Food. Different tabs, same account, one tap apart.

  2. Show up. Get rewarded.

    The more consistent you get, the more it pays. Every distinct day you train in a week climbs the ladder — 10, 20, 40, 80, up to 100 coins. Reset every week.

    Coins buy themes, card styles and avatars.

  3. Ghost Mode or Friends?

    Ghost Mode is the default, and it means what it says: everything stays on this device. Private, fast, and yours alone. Turn on Friends and the only thing that ever leaves is your profile card. Never a workout, never a meal.

    Exactly what's on that card →

  4. Start from a premade, or build it yourself

    Open a blank session and fill it in as you train, or start from a preset you've already built. No inspiration today? The premade catalog has whole sessions ready to add in one tap. Built by our team here at Agil, by hand. No AI.

    Log reps and weight set by set, keep per-exercise notes and target rep ranges, and run the rest timer between sets. Tt still alerts you with the phone locked. Unilateral lifts are tracked per side rather than averaged into one number.

  5. An app that's yours

    Build a character, pick a card style, showcase the badges you care about. The shop rotates daily, and everything in it is cosmetic. Nothing you can equip changes what the app tracks.

  6. A rank that moves on consistency, not on how much you lift

    The Strategist ladder runs pawn to king. It climbs on showing up, so it rewards the part of training that's actually hard.

And on the food side

The other half of the app

Food and macros

Log meals against calorie and macro goals, with fiber, sugar and sodium tracked alongside protein, carbs and fat. Can't seem to remember to take your creatine? Agils got you covered. Pick a focus nutrient and Agil can remind you to keep you on track.

Barcode scanning

Point the camera at a barcode and the food comes back with its full nutrition breakdown, sourced from the Open Food Facts database. Anything it doesn't know, you can add by hand and keep in your own library.

Progress worth reading

Charts and trends from everything you've logged: training volume, your best lifts, estimated one-rep maxes, and how the whole thing is moving over months rather than days. A summary of what you've achieved, always private.

Achievements and the badge book

Badges land quietly and wait for you in a paged book, sealed until you open them. Thresholds are calibrated to you when you first set the app up, so they mean something whatever you're lifting.

Home screen widget

Today's calories and your focus goal on the home screen, drawn in whatever theme the app is currently wearing.

Friends, only if you want them

Share a five-character friend code. With this you can share recipes, workouts, or just an exercise you're trying.

Nothing is behind a paywall

Agil got built because every fitness app I tried put the useful half behind a subscription, or all of the workouts and progression was tracked by AI. With Agil, I could write my vision into it: always free software that grows with you without selling your data.

Your training data isn't the product either. Workouts, food, water and bodyweight stay in the app's own storage on your phone unless you deliberately leave Ghost Mode, and even then, all that leaves is your profile card. privacy policy spells out exactly what that includes.

For the curious

How it's built

Written by one person — a SwiftUI app and a Rust service, no framework scaffolding underneath either of them.

iOS app

SwiftUI, iOS 16.1 and up. The project is generated from a project.yml with XcodeGen rather than a checked-in .xcodeproj, so the target graph is reviewable in a diff.

  • Swift
  • SwiftUI
  • WidgetKit
  • AVFoundation
  • XcodeGen

Theming

A theme is a Codable struct of hex strings, so custom themes persist as JSON like everything else. Adaptive themes resolve to a dynamic UIColor that switches with the system appearance, and the typeface rides along on the theme via one root .fontDesign() that the whole view tree inherits.

Storage

One type touches the disk. Everything is Codable JSON written with a schema version wrapped around it, so migrations are possible later without corrupting existing saves. The widget reads today's numbers out of a shared App Group.

The shop has no backend

The daily rotation is computed offline from a date seed, so every user sees the same featured items on the same day with no server round trip and no way for the app to phone home about what you're browsing.

Backend

Rust on Axum with Diesel over SQLite. It serves the shared profile cards and the friend graph, and proxies food lookups to Open Food Facts as a read-through cache so the same barcode is only ever fetched upstream once.

  • Rust
  • Axum
  • Diesel
  • SQLite

Identity without accounts

There is no email, no password and no user table. A device generates a random UUID and a 256-bit secret held in the Keychain; the server stores only the SHA-256 of that secret and trusts the first write to claim the id. Reads of a card are public, writes must present the key.

Abuse limits

A hand-rolled per-IP token bucket, one bucket per route class, so hammering food search can't lock out card sync. The bucket map sweeps itself so it can't become the memory-exhaustion surface it was meant to prevent. Friend requests get a separate per-user daily quota that counts attempts, not successes, to bound code enumeration.

This page

Hand-written HTML and CSS with no build step and no framework. The scroll tour is anime.js, vendored as a file rather than pulled from a CDN, and it drives one class on <html>, everything it does is additive, so the page is complete without it.
